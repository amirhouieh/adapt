# Judgment of stored understanding over time — Qwen, v1 versus v2

Updated after the user's clarification: judge what exists inside learners' understanding, excluding query answers. This supersedes the earlier combined memory-and-answer comparison. Raw collection files are unchanged; no new model runs or production changes were made.

## Conclusion

**Stored understanding shows specific v2 gains, but no consistent overall improvement in learning quality over time over v1 on either Qwen model.** V1's coherent synthesis sometimes invents an explanation or hardens into an obsolete one. This v2 configuration often keeps more concrete context, but can leave fragments, contradictions, unrelated deletions, or stalled learning.

The earlier claims that v2 is better because its public answers are more useful, or that v1 is worse because an answer is empty, are excluded. Following a changed query scope also earns no credit here. A learner can hold useful understanding and answer badly, or hold weak understanding and answer fluently.

## Basis of judgment

The user's sole principle remains learning quality over time. Evidence consists of the original input stream, committed understanding at checkpoints/final state, v1 synthesis transitions, and v2 committed history with wire updates used to trace where a memory change originated. A generated candidate counts as learning only when reflected in persistent understanding. The question is whether the evolving understanding meaningfully integrates experience, remains grounded, and revises its conclusions when circumstances change. No scores, weights, perfect-recall requirement, or extra acceptance threshold were introduced.
